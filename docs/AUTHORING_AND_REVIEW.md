# Authoring and reviewer guide

Every item needs one clear purpose, an answer or rubric, a complete solution, expected time, difficulty rationale, exam-version fit, topic/skill tags, and licensing metadata. Distractors should represent plausible misconceptions, not random values. Avoid trick wording unless the target exam explicitly assesses it.

Reviewers preview rendered math/assets, solve independently, compare the keyed answer and explanation, inspect validation results and similarity candidates, check source/license evidence, and leave specific notes. Subject and editorial approval are distinct. A reviewer must reject or flag any item that resembles protected content too closely or makes an unsupported factual claim.

Generated content is never official. Do not prompt a model to imitate, rewrite, or minimally transform a named official question. A generated similar-practice request uses a structured skill specification and enters the ordinary draft queue.
